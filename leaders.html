Who backs the party leaders?

How often each member votes with their party's leader in their chamber (Hakeem Jeffries in the House, Chuck Schumer in the Senate), how often they break away, and how much money the leaders' committees gave them. The public's view and the establishment's don't always line up; this shows where each member sits.

The telling votes

Agreement with the leader when Democrats split

On most votes nearly every Democrat votes the same way, so overall agreement is high for everyone. The votes where at least one Democrat in ten voted the other way show who sides with leadership when it counts.

What the columns mean

How it's measured

Same vote as leaderShare of roll calls where both the member and the leader voted Yea or Nay and voted the same way. From the official House Clerk and senate.gov records.
When Democrats splitThe same, counting only votes where at least 10% of Democrats voted against the rest of the party.
Votes with most DemocratsParty unity: how often the member voted with the Democratic majority, and their rank among Democrats in their chamber (1 = most often). Ties share a rank.
Money from leadersContributions from party leaders' campaign committees, leadership PACs and party campaign committees (DCCC, DSCC), from FEC filings, all cycles tracked.
Establishment verdictThe headline on each member's Establishment page, which states the rule it follows. Not every tracker has one yet.
Each member's figures come from their own tracker, where every vote and contribution links to its source.
