About

Receipts, not opinions.

Congress Receipts is a set of nonpartisan trackers for members of Congress: how they vote, who funds them, whether they show up, and whether they keep their promises. Every number links to the official record it came from.

What this is

Each member gets a tracker with the same sections: votes, money, party alignment, stances with evidence, a promises scorecard, ties to the political establishment, town halls and statements, plus a Sources page that lists every claim and how it was checked. Because every tracker is built the same way, members can be compared fairly.

The Backlog Queue shows every current member of Congress and which ones have a tracker so far.

Who makes it

An independent project researched, built and maintained by one person. It is not affiliated with any party, campaign, member of Congress or advocacy group, and nothing here is an endorsement of anyone. It has no ads and no tracking cookies.

How we check

Every claim has a receipt

Official records firstVotes come from the House Clerk and senate.gov, money from FEC filings, bills from govinfo, and words from members' own press releases and archived campaign sites.
Built, not typedVote tallies, percentages and dollar figures are computed from the raw records by scripts, so they can't be mistyped.
Checked against the sourceEvery quote, vote and figure is re-checked against its source. A misquote, a wrong vote or a wrong tally fails the check, and the data is fixed, never the checker.
Verdicts state their ruleWhere a page sums up a record ("Mostly no", "Mixed"), it says the rule that produced it, and shows every item behind it.

Where the data comes from

  • House Clerk roll calls and Senate roll calls for votes and attendance
  • Federal Election Commission filings for money
  • govinfo and congress.gov for bill records
  • Census Bureau for districts
  • Members' own public statements, websites and archived campaign sites, and published news reports

It uses no private, leaked or purchased data. Donor names and addresses are never published, only employer and occupation.

What it is not

Summaries and verdicts are this project's reading of public records, each with its stated rule; the linked source is always the official record. Information is provided as-is for public education and may lag the latest filings. If you find a mistake, the fix goes into the data, with the source.
